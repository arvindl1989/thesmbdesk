The first 100 customers are the hardest, and they are almost never won with
advertising. At that stage you do not yet know which message works, so paid
ads mostly buy you expensive proof that you were wrong.

What works instead is unglamorous: being findable, being asked for, and being
present where your buyers already are. Here are eleven ways to do that, ranked
roughly by how much return they produce per hour spent for a typical Indian
small business.

**If you only do one thing this week, do number 1.**

## 1. Claim and finish your Google Business Profile

For any business a customer could plausibly visit, phone, or search locally,
this is the single highest-return hour you will spend. It is free, it puts you
in Maps and the local pack, and most competitors fill it in halfway and never
touch it again.

Finishing it properly means: correct category, real photos (not stock),
accurate hours including holidays, services listed individually, and a phone
number that someone actually answers.

**Start today:** add ten genuine photos and ask your three most recent happy
customers for a review. Reviews are the ranking factor most within your
control, and the fastest way to get them is to ask directly, with a link, the
day after you have delivered something well.

## 2. Turn WhatsApp into a storefront, not just a chat

WhatsApp is where Indian commerce actually happens. WhatsApp Business — the
free app, not the paid API — gives you a catalogue, quick replies, labels for
organising chats, and an away message.

The catalogue matters more than people expect. It converts "what do you have?"
into a browsable list you send in one tap, which shortens the distance between
enquiry and order.

**Start today:** put your top ten products or services in the catalogue with
prices, and write three quick replies for the questions you answer daily.

## 3. Ask your first ten customers for one introduction each

Referral is the highest-converting channel in existence and the most
under-asked. Most business owners never ask, because it feels awkward. It is
much less awkward than it feels — especially right after you have delivered
something the customer is happy with.

Be specific. "Do you know anyone who might need this?" produces nothing.
"You mentioned your cousin runs a printing business — would you introduce me?"
produces a customer.

**Start today:** list ten customers who were genuinely pleased. Message each
one individually, naming a specific person or type of person.

## 4. Get listed where B2B buyers already search

If you sell to businesses, IndiaMART, TradeIndia and JustDial are where a
large share of Indian procurement still begins. The lead quality is uneven and
you will get time-wasters — but the free listings cost you nothing except the
effort of responding fast.

Speed is the whole game on these platforms. The supplier who replies in ten
minutes wins the enquiry the supplier who replies tomorrow never sees.

**Start today:** create the free listings and set a notification that you will
actually notice.

## 5. Show up where your buyers already gather

Trade associations, local business groups, industry WhatsApp communities,
market associations, and sector-specific meetups. Not to pitch — to be known.
In Indian B2B especially, buying still runs substantially on relationships and
personal vouching.

The rule is to be the most useful person in the group rather than the loudest.
Answer questions in your area of expertise without attaching a sales pitch,
consistently, for a few months. Business follows.

**Start today:** identify three groups where your buyers actually are, join
them, and answer one question in each.

## 6. Use LinkedIn properly if you sell to businesses

For B2B services, organic LinkedIn remains unusually effective for Indian
founders, because most of your competitors post nothing. You do not need a
following of thousands — you need the right two hundred people to recognise
your name.

Post about the work: a problem a client had, how you approached it, what you
would do differently. Specific beats generic, and a real anecdote beats a
listicle of tips every time.

**Start today:** write one post about a real problem you solved this month.

## 7. Answer the questions your buyers type into Google

Every business has a set of questions customers ask before buying — about
price, process, compliance, comparison. Each of those is a search someone is
performing right now, and a page you could own.

This is slower than the other tactics here; expect months, not weeks. But it
compounds in a way nothing else on this list does, and it keeps working while
you sleep.

**Start today:** write down the ten questions you answer most often on calls.
Those are your first ten articles. Our
[email marketing guide](/blog/email-marketing-guide-small-business/) is an
example of the format — one comprehensive answer to a question people are
actively searching.

## 8. Partner with a business that already has your customers

Find a business that serves the same customer but sells something different —
the wedding photographer and the caterer, the CA and the business consultant,
the interior designer and the furniture shop. Each of you has an audience the
other wants, and neither is a competitor.

This is the fastest way to borrow trust you have not yet earned.

**Start today:** name five non-competing businesses with your customer.
Approach one with a specific proposal, not a vague "let's collaborate".

## 9. Publish in the language your customers actually use

English-first content is the default and, for many Indian SMBs, a mistake.
Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali and Gujarati searches have grown
sharply, and the competition for those queries is a fraction of English.

If your customers speak a regional language, publishing in it is one of the
few remaining places where good content still ranks quickly.

**Start today:** translate your single best page — properly, by a human, not
machine-dumped.

## 10. Do free work in public

Not spec work for a client. Public work: a teardown of a well-known company's
process, a free audit you publish, a template you give away, a walkthrough of
how you would approach a common problem.

This demonstrates competence instead of claiming it, which matters enormously
when you have no track record to point at yet.

**Start today:** publish one useful artefact — a checklist, a calculator, a
template — that a potential customer would keep.

## 11. Make every invoice a touchpoint

You already send invoices. Almost nobody uses them as a marketing surface.

Add a line asking for a Google review. Add a referral offer. Capture the email
address if you do not have it. The invoice is opened by definition — it is the
one message you send that everybody reads.

**Start today:** add a review link and a referral line to your invoice
template. If your invoicing tool makes that awkward, see our comparison of

## Track it, or none of this is repeatable

The point of the first 100 customers is not the revenue — it is learning which
channel produces customers you can actually serve profitably. That only works
if you record where each one came from.

You do not need software for this on day one. A spreadsheet with name, source,
date and outcome is enough. When it stops being enough, our guide to

covers the next step.

At 100 customers you will find that two or three of these eleven produced most
of your business. That is the actual output of this exercise: not a hundred
customers, but knowing which three things to do a thousand more times.
