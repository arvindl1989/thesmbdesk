Most Indian small businesses do not lose deals because they lack a CRM. They
lose deals because a WhatsApp message got buried, a callback never happened,
and a quote sat unsent for nine days. A CRM is worth adopting only if it
closes that specific gap — and a free plan is the sensible place to find out
whether it will.

This guide covers seven CRMs with genuinely usable free tiers, what each one
is actually good at, and the point at which each stops being free in practice.

## Why free CRM plans matter here

The typical Indian SMB sales process runs on three things: a phone, WhatsApp,
and someone's memory. That works up to roughly 20–30 open enquiries. Past
that, follow-up leakage becomes the single largest source of lost revenue —
and it is invisible, because nobody logs the deal they forgot to chase.

Free CRM plans matter because they let you test whether structured follow-up
actually changes your close rate before committing budget. If it does, paying
later is easy to justify. If it does not, you have lost nothing.

What free plans are *not* is a permanent solution for a growing team. Every
vendor on this list designs its free tier as a funnel into a paid one. That is
fine, as long as you go in knowing where the walls are.

## How I evaluated these

I did not run a controlled trial of seven CRMs across a live sales team, and
you should be suspicious of any article that claims to have. What follows is
an assessment of each product's free tier against the criteria that actually
determine whether a small Indian business sticks with a CRM past month two.

Those criteria, in the order they tend to matter:

1. **WhatsApp reality.** In India, WhatsApp is the sales channel. A CRM that
   cannot log, template, or trigger WhatsApp conversations is fighting the way
   your customers already behave.
2. **Mobile usability.** The owner or field rep updates records from a phone,
   standing in a corridor, or the records do not get updated at all.
3. **Import and export freedom.** You are starting from a spreadsheet, and you
   must be able to leave with your data intact. Restricted export is the
   single most expensive trap in this category.
4. **Contact and user ceilings.** Free tiers cap either seats or records.
   Which one binds first depends entirely on your business shape.
5. **Quoting and invoicing adjacency.** Rupee amounts, GST-aware quotes, and a
   clean handoff to whatever raises the actual invoice.
6. **Time to first value.** If it takes a fortnight of configuration, an
   eight-person business will abandon it.

Vendor free-tier limits change often — sometimes quarterly. Treat the shape of
each recommendation below as durable and **confirm the current numbers on the
vendor's own pricing page before you commit**.

## The seven

### 1. Zoho Bigin — the default for most small Indian teams

Bigin is Zoho's deliberately stripped-down, pipeline-first CRM, built for
exactly the business that found full Zoho CRM overwhelming. It is opinionated
in a useful way: deals move through stages, and the interface makes the next
action obvious.

Its strongest argument is adjacency. If you already use Zoho Invoice, Books,
or Mail, Bigin sits in the same account with the same data, and the handoff
from won deal to GST invoice is close to frictionless. Indian pricing and
rupee-native billing are a given rather than an afterthought.

**Choose it if:** you want a CRM that a non-technical team will actually use,
and you are already anywhere near the Zoho ecosystem.

**Where it breaks:** deliberately limited automation. It is not built for
complex multi-team pipelines, and it is not trying to be.

### 2. HubSpot CRM Free — the most generous ceiling

HubSpot's free tier is unusually unrestricted on contacts, which makes it the
obvious pick when you have a large list and a small team. The interface is
polished, the learning curve is gentle, and the free tier includes email
tracking and basic deal pipelines.

The catch is the upgrade cliff. HubSpot's paid tiers are priced for
well-funded companies, and in rupee terms the jump from free to Starter to
Professional is steep. Features you will want — real automation, meaningful
reporting — sit above the free line.

**Choose it if:** you have thousands of contacts, a couple of users, and
mostly need organisation rather than automation.

**Where it breaks:** the moment you need workflow automation, the price stops
being SMB-friendly in an Indian cost context.

### 3. Freshsales — the strongest Indian-built option

Freshworks is an Indian company, and it shows in the product decisions:
telephony and conversational channels are treated as first-class rather than
bolted on. The free tier covers contact and account management with a
reasonable mobile app.

**Choose it if:** your sales motion is phone-heavy, which describes a large
share of Indian B2B and high-consideration B2C.

**Where it breaks:** the free tier is narrow. You will meet a paywall faster
here than with HubSpot.

### 4. Bitrix24 — the most features per rupee, at a cost

Bitrix24's free plan is remarkable in scope: CRM, tasks, chat, telephony, even
a website builder, with generous user counts. If raw capability per zero
rupees is the metric, nothing else is close.

The cost is complexity. Bitrix24 is a large, dense product, and it demands
real setup effort. Small teams frequently install it, find it overwhelming,
and quietly revert to spreadsheets.

**Choose it if:** you have someone willing to own configuration, and you want
CRM plus internal collaboration in one place.

**Where it breaks:** time to first value. Budget days, not hours.

### 5. EngageBay — CRM plus marketing in one free tier

EngageBay bundles CRM, email marketing, and basic automation into a single
free plan, priced aggressively for smaller markets. For a business that wants
one tool rather than a CRM plus a separate email platform, the consolidation
is genuinely valuable.

**Choose it if:** you want contacts and email campaigns under one roof and
your list is still modest.

**Where it breaks:** email sending limits on the free tier arrive quickly, and
it is a smaller vendor than the others here.

### 6. Odoo CRM — the right answer if you will outgrow CRM entirely

Odoo's free CRM app is a doorway into a full ERP. If you can foresee needing
inventory, purchasing, manufacturing, or accounting under one system, starting
in Odoo avoids a painful migration in two years.

**Choose it if:** you are a product business whose real problem is operations,
not just sales follow-up.

**Where it breaks:** single-app free usage is the constraint, and Odoo
generally needs a partner or a technical person to configure well.

### 7. Agile CRM — a lightweight fallback

Agile CRM offers a small-team free tier covering contacts, deals, and basic
marketing features. It is not the strongest product here, but it is simple and
unintimidating.

**Choose it if:** you want the smallest possible step up from a spreadsheet.

**Where it breaks:** limited depth, and development pace has been slower than
its competitors.

## Which one, by business shape

| If you are | Start with | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A solo consultant or freelancer | Zoho Bigin | Fastest to useful, cheap to grow into |
| A 2–5 person B2B sales team | Freshsales or Bigin | Phone-first workflow, Indian pricing |
| Sitting on a large contact list | HubSpot Free | Contact ceiling will not bind |
| Wanting CRM + email in one | EngageBay | Avoids a second subscription |
| A product business with inventory | Odoo | Grows into operations, not just sales |
| Willing to invest setup time | Bitrix24 | Most capability at zero cost |
| Barely past spreadsheets | Agile CRM | Lowest intimidation factor |

## The three questions that matter more than the shortlist

**Can you get your data out?** Before importing a single contact, find the
export function and run it. If a CRM makes leaving difficult, that difficulty
is a feature from the vendor's perspective, not a bug.

**Who updates it, and when?** A CRM nobody updates is worse than a spreadsheet
nobody updates, because it costs money and creates false confidence. Decide
the update ritual — end of each call, end of each day — before you decide the
tool.

**What breaks first, seats or records?** If you have three salespeople and 500
contacts, seat limits bind. If you have one owner and 40,000 contacts, record
limits bind. Match the free tier's shape to yours.

## Verdict

For most Indian small businesses reading this, **Zoho Bigin** is the right
starting point: it is built for small teams, priced for this market, and its
adjacency to GST-compliant invoicing removes a real operational seam. If your
constraint is a large contact list rather than a large team, start with
**HubSpot Free** instead. If your sales run on phone calls, look at
**Freshsales** first.

Whichever you pick, commit to eight weeks of consistent use before judging it.
The value of a CRM is not visible in week one — it shows up as the deals you
stop forgetting.
